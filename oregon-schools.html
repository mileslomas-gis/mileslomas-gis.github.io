Oregon Schools:
Poverty & Academic Performance

Examining school-level associations between student poverty and achievement in science, mathematics, and English language arts.

Project Overview

This project analyzes Oregon Department of Education school report card data to explore relationships among student demographics, attendance, staffing, and academic performance. The analysis moves from data preparation and exploration to hypothesis testing and predictive modeling.

Dataset

School-level records from the Oregon Department of Education, including academic outcomes and demographic indicators.

Tools

Python, Pandas, NumPy, Seaborn, Matplotlib, Statsmodels, and Scikit-learn.

Evaluation

Regression slopes, p-values, confidence intervals, residual plots, and training and holdout root mean squared error.

Analytical Workflow

Data Preparation

Standardized numeric and percentage fields, addressed missing values, and assigned representative values to certain suppressed ranges.

Exploration & Inference

Summarized school and county patterns, calculated Pearson correlations, and used ordinary least squares regression to estimate associations and uncertainty.

Predictive Modeling

Compared simple linear, multiple linear, and quadratic regression models using training and holdout RMSE.

Findings & Interpretation

The notebook identifies negative associations between the proportion of students experiencing poverty at a school and the proportions meeting academic standards in the three subjects. It also examines whether additional predictors and quadratic terms improve predictive performance.

These are observational, school-level relationships. They do not establish that poverty causes an individual student’s academic outcome. Missing records, approximated suppressed values, and differences among schools affect interpretation. Model comparisons use random train/holdout splits and should be treated as exploratory.
